# About

<p class="status">Based in Dallas · open to relocation</p>

I'm Senthen: a lifelong learner and an aspiring scientist, inventor, and engineer. You could argue I'm already all three. I finished my B.S. in Computer Science at the University of Texas at Dallas, and I'm now working on an M.S. through Georgia Tech's OMSCS program. It's a fun way to keep learning while I look for the right role.

## Why technology

As a kid I believed, and I still do, that technology is where real creation happens. In a lot of fields, what gets called innovation is often an old technique that was forgotten and rediscovered, or a mashup of ideas that adds up to less than its parts. Technology recombines too, but its combinations tend to compound: each one becomes a building block for the next. That's how, in the modern sense, it lets us do more with less.

## Why systems

Systems thinking came later, from one question: what's left once writing code by hand is automated? I wrote about where that led in [What's left when the typing is automated]({{ '/notes/when-typing-is-automated/' | relative_url }}).

## How I work

I try to work from first principles, figuring out why something is true before deciding what to build on top of it. I like fast, honest feedback loops, so I find out quickly whether something actually works. And I lean toward parsimony: the simplest thing that does the job.

I'm drawn to problems that take real thought. I'm better at recognizing a pattern or an answer than recalling one cold. I can be slow to warm up on a project, but once I do, I get a little obsessed.

There's a saying: "A jack of all trades is a master of none, but oftentimes better than a master of one." That's the kind of engineer I want to be.

## Interests

- **Systems thinking.** Feedback, delay, and structure as the causes of behavior, drawing on cybernetics, control theory, and system dynamics. This is the lens I bring to everything below.
- **Distributed systems.** Concurrency, networking, and what happens when parts fail independently: consistency, partial failure, and resilience.
- **Operating systems.** Processes, memory, scheduling, and the boundary between a program and the machine it runs on.
- **Databases.** Storage, indexing, transactions, and the trade-offs behind keeping data correct and fast.
- **Software delivery.** The SDLC as a system: testing, CI/CD, and the feedback loops that let teams ship safely.
