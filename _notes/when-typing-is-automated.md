---
title: "What's left when the typing is automated"
date: 2026-10-15
---

My interest in systems thinking started with one question: what will be left when manual coding gets automated?

I made a few assumptions, and they're coming true. Tools like Claude and GPT, colloquially called AI, are amazing mimicry artists, and they can do a lot of great things. Manual typing is becoming something you do in emergencies.

But they can create something without understanding the drawbacks and benefits of the decisions they made. They copy like a parrot, or blend in like a chameleon.

So the bottleneck isn't writing code anymore. It's how effective the decision-making is, and how clearly the entire architecture is thought out, drawbacks included.

That question sent me down the rabbit hole of systems. With the help of *Thinking in Systems*, I came to see a revolution within the AI revolution: the sciences reaching the limits of reductionism, which is bringing holism and systems thinking back into the light. None of this is a new idea in the scientific community. A lot of Hegelian dynamics come into play here: thesis, antithesis, synthesis, and dualities like order and chaos, or creation and destruction.

If the parts are getting cheap to produce, the value is in how they fit together.
