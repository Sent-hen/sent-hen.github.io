---
title: "What's left when the typing is automated"
date: 2026-10-15
---

My interest in systems thinking started with one question: what's left when writing code by hand gets automated?

I made a few guesses back then, and they're coming true. Tools like Claude and GPT, what most people just call AI, are amazing mimicry artists. They can do a lot of great things, and typing out code by hand is turning into something you only do in an emergency.

But they can build something without understanding the trade-offs of the decisions they made. They copy like a parrot, or blend in like a chameleon. So the bottleneck is shifting away from writing code and toward how good the decisions are, and how clearly the whole architecture is thought through, drawbacks included.

That's what pulled me into systems. Reading Donella Meadows's *Thinking in Systems*, I started to see a revolution inside the AI revolution: the sciences are running into the limits of reductionism, and that's bringing holism and systems thinking back into the light. None of these ideas are new to the scientific community; they're just coming back around. It all feels very Hegelian: thesis, antithesis, synthesis, with dualities like order and chaos, or creation and destruction, playing out again.

If the parts are getting cheap to make, the value is in how they fit together.
